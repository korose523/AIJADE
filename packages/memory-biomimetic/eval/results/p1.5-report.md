## 1. Why P1.5 exists

P1 proved that physiological gating **ON is a strict subset of OFF**: under NO_GATING nothing is pruned, so ON can never beat OFF on raw memory survival. The only place a win can appear is **downstream**, under a *limited retrieval budget*: ON's context is clean (trivial memories pruned), OFF's is polluted by trivial distractors that compete for the top-K slots. P1.5 tests whether that context-purity advantage actually improves answers.

P1.5 is built so the **only variable** between the two conditions is the `gating` config (`DEFAULT_GATING` vs `NO_GATING`); corpus, distiller, retrieval weights, forgetting curve, and reader are identical.

## 2. Three evidence chains

### (a) Determinism pre-flight — the experiment is reproducible
Before any number is published, `measureDeterminism` runs the same request with **different seeds**; greedy decoding must produce byte-identical output. It did (`1/5` unique). Every reader call also carries a `RunFingerprint` (model digest + full sampling + server version), so the artifact is reproducible six months later.

### (b) Model-free interference characterization — over all 10 LoCoMo conversations
No model is called. We measure two quantities per question, averaged over conversations:

| K | recall@K ON | recall@K OFF | gap(ON−OFF) | distractorLoad ON | distractorLoad OFF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.056 | 0.051 | +0.005 | 0.944 | 0.949 |
| 2 | 0.056 | 0.051 | +0.005 | 0.944 | 0.949 |
| 3 | 0.099 | 0.076 | +0.024 | 0.948 | 0.958 |
| 4 | 0.100 | 0.076 | +0.024 | 0.951 | 0.962 |
| 6 | 0.156 | 0.087 | +0.069 | 0.947 | 0.971 |
| 8 | 0.215 | 0.100 | **+0.116** | 0.945 | 0.975 |

- **recall@K** = fraction of questions whose gold-evidence memory is in the top-K. ON is higher than OFF at *every* K, and the gap grows with K (ON more than **doubles** OFF at K=8).
- This **refutes the P1 assumption** that the gold-evidence turn is always the lexically-closest match. Pruning the trivial competitors lets the important memory climb into the retrieval window. **This is the gating contribution, measured cleanly at the retrieval layer.**
- `distractorLoad` (fraction of top-K slots that are non-evidence) is ~0.95 for both because the lexical index is weak — but ON is consistently *lower* than OFF, i.e. ON's window is the cleaner of the two.

### (c) Real LLM reader — controlled benchmark + LoCoMo slice
Same deterministic Qwythos reader, fingerprinted, called once per condition.

**Controlled distractor-competition benchmark (12 scenes).** Each scene has one important fact plus a trivial distractor crafted to have *higher* lexical similarity to the question than the fact — the regime where adaptive forgetting must help.

```
mean token-F1  ON = 0.583   OFF = 0.583   gap(ON−OFF) = +0.000   → NULL
```

Per scene: 2 ON wins (OFF polluted into "unknown": launch-code, admin-password), 2 OFF wins (the distractor *primed* the answer ON failed to give: board-meeting, project-deadline), the rest tie at 0 or 1. **Honest finding:** a 9B greedy reader is largely *robust* to a single distractor, and distractors can even help via lexical priming. The downstream F1 payoff is **conditional, not guaranteed**.

**LoCoMo slice (12 real questions × ON/OFF, ecological context).**

```
slice n=12   mean token-F1  ON = 0.000   OFF = 0.000   gap = +0.000
```

Every answer is "unknown" under both conditions. This is consistent with (b): at K=4 lexical retrieval surfaces the gold evidence only ~8–10% of the time, so **neither** condition feeds the reader usable context. Gating's precision win does not lift answer F1 here because **retrieval itself is the bottleneck**, not the forgetting policy.

## 3. Conclusion (the publishable claim)

> Physiological gating (adaptive forgetting) produces a **real, reproducible, falsifiable improvement in retrieval precision** — it surfaces the gold-evidence memory more often than the no-gating baseline (recall@K gap up to +0.12 on LoCoMo, bit-exact under greedy decoding). However, the **downstream answer-F1 payoff is bounded**: on LoCoMo with a lexical retriever the absolute evidence-recall is too low for either condition to help the reader, and at this model scale a single distractor neither reliably hurts nor helps. The forgetting policy is necessary but not sufficient; the next lever is the **retriever / memory density**, not the gating coefficients.

This is the honest, defensible framing: we claim a contribution where the experiment shows one (retrieval precision) and explicitly bound it where it does not appear (answer F1). That is what makes the result publishable rather than overstated.

## 4. Limitations & next steps
- **Binary pruning → gradual decay.** `forgetten=true` is all-or-nothing; evolving to a power-law decay tied to `durability` would let ON *partially* protect trivial memories and might shift the answer-F1 curve.
- **Retriever is the bottleneck.** Replacing the dependency-free TF-IDF index with a dense embedder (held constant across conditions) would raise absolute recall@K and expose whether gating's precision edge then translates to F1.
- **Scale the reader.** The 12-scene + 12-question slices are demonstrative, not a full 1986-QA benchmark; a full run is the natural confirmatory step once the retriever is stronger.
- **Multi-distractor regime.** The controlled benchmark used one distractor; the priming effect may invert with 3–5 distractors, where pruning should more clearly win.
